A Sovereign Website proposal about TauGuard, built from TauGuard's public pages. Not TauGuard Limited's own site. Design 4 of 5: Briefing.

Two companies, one boundary

TauGuard governs what may happen. RiskMandate writes down what each agent may do, and puts something in the way of the gap. Both are about the same place: the line between an agent's intent and its consequence.

TauGuard

Governs what may happen

TauGuard says: "Intelligence may propose. Deterministic governance evaluates admissibility. Execution occurs only when the governing conditions are satisfied."

Source: tauguard.ai/governance.html, read 24 Nov 2026.
RiskMandate

Writes down what each agent may do

RiskMandate says it can "tell you what each one can actually do, write down what you authorised it to do, and put something in the way of the difference."

Source: riskmandate.ai, read 24 Nov 2026.

What TauGuard builds

One company, several systems that are not one product. Everything on this page is TauGuard's own description, read on 24 Nov 2026, with the page it came from.

One company, multiple architectures

TauGuard says it is "the company", and that its systems "are not interchangeable implementations of one product": "ARCHONT GOS and TauDIL GOS are separate products. TauDIL GOS is not a renamed ARCHONT GOS component, and ARCHONT GOS is not a replacement for TauDIL GOS."

Source: tauguard.ai/systems.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

ARCHONT GOS

A Governance Operating System for organisational state and change. TauGuard says it maintains "the governed relationship between purpose, authority, meaning, canonical knowledge, rules, evidence, dependencies, assessments, current state and operational consequence". It includes TauMesh and TauStamp.

Source: tauguard.ai/archon.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

TauDIL GOS

A separate Governance Operating System for intelligent systems and intelligence-producing processes: deterministic intelligence governance, semantic coherence and semantic drift, "with SYGON integrated into the architecture".

Source: tauguard.ai/taudil.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

SYGON

Described as a semantic governance layer that does not generate the answer. TauGuard's line: "When AI does not know, it should know not to invent." Its four steps: ground, context, evaluate, decide (allow or refuse).

Source: tauguard.ai/sygon.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

ROD

Gamified PC security "designed to make protective behaviour visible, understandable and engaging". TauGuard says: "The system is under development." We say no more than that.

Source: tauguard.ai/rod.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

IEL Guard

TauGuard's page says IEL Guard "enforces AI behavioral constraints with cryptographic proof", in three steps: define invariants, enforce on every action, anchor proof on-chain. The page states "100% action coverage" and "under 1ms enforcement latency" as figures; we have not tested either. TauGuard's own systems overview lists three systems and links IEL from its menu, so we show it as a fourth item and say so.

Source: tauguard.ai/iel.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

Frameworks, shown apart

TauGuard says the frameworks "are distinct from TauGuard products. A framework establishes a research or architectural model; a system implements capabilities for a particular governed environment." It lists five: IFA (Intelligence From Architecture), SFA (Security From Architecture), GFA (Governance From Architecture), AGL (Authority Governed Learning) and ALA (Admissible Learning Architecture).

Source: tauguard.ai/frameworks.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

Its governance page also names GOM (Governance of Meaning), GUD (Governance Under Degradation) and TauMesh (topological governance) as "governance extensions".

Source: tauguard.ai/governance.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

IFA is published as "Core Specification v1.0".

Source: tauguard.ai/ifa.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

What we have not said

We name nobody at TauGuard. We use no TauGuard logo or branding. Where TauGuard states a number, we repeat it as its claim, not as something we checked.

Their architecture, drawn

Six of TauGuard's own designs as diagrams. The words in the boxes are theirs; the drawings are ours, so check each against its source before relying on it.

1. The governance flow

TauGuard says every execution request "passes through a deterministic verification path": input (request, identity, context, evidence), meaning (governed semantic interpretation), evaluation (constitutional predicates execute), admissibility (authority and constraints verified), decision, and chronicle (evidence and lineage committed).

Source: tauguard.ai/governance.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

2. Intelligence advises, the core decides, the boundary holds

"Authority is not intelligence." Intelligence may propose; the deterministic core holds the decision; consequence sits behind the execution boundary. Of structural refusal (GOV-04) TauGuard says: "refusal is terminal rather than an invitation to retry around the constraint."

Source: tauguard.ai/governance.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

3. ARCHONT's six layers and its execution seam

TauGuard says ARCHONT "governs the relationship between organizational authority and operational exchange. It does not need to replace every ERP, AI system, API, machine or workflow that performs operational work."

Source: tauguard.ai/architecture.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

4. TauMesh: five deltas and the lifecycle

"A change is local in origin, but its consequences are determined by topology." A material change is evaluated through dependencies and can invalidate what was admissible before.

Source: tauguard.ai/architecture.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

5. TauDIL's flow

TauDIL separates generating intelligence from evaluating it. SYGON is "integrated into TauDIL GOS as part of its semantic governance capability stack".

Source: tauguard.ai/taudil.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

6. IFA's seven-layer stack

TauGuard's IFA page sets out "seven layers forming the constitutional execution stack. Every action passes through each layer in sequence." It places SYGON at layer 2, AGL at layer 3 and IEL at layer 5, and says the chronicle "is written before action, not after".

Source: tauguard.ai/ifa.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

Two decision vocabularies. The governance page gives three outcomes: APPROVE, REVIEW, BLOCK. The IFA page describes AGL's admission as four: ALLOW, RESTRICT, DEFER, BLOCK. We use the governance page's three throughout this site and have not worked out how the two relate. TauGuard should say which is current.

Not drawn here: the Governance Under Degradation states, G0 (integral) to G5 (recovery), on the governance page. We have not yet worked out what they mean for the workflows.

RiskMandate

RiskMandate's facts, as it states them, with its sources. We add nothing to the numbers and we repeat what it says was not done.

The ABP

An Agent Behaviour Policy "describes one AI agent in one deployment: everything it can do (the grant), what you actually authorised (the mandate), the gap between the two (the delta), and what really stands in the way of each thing (the barrier). It describes and it does not judge, so it carries no score."

Source: riskmandate.ai/agent-behaviour-policy.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

On its own team's pages the same four terms are Reach, Mandate, Gap and Barrier, and each barrier is labelled "a rule the agent keeps, a review by another agent or person, or a technical control the agent cannot change".

Source: web-summit.sgit.ai/abp/in-action/, read 24 Nov 2026.

Policy, hooks, boundary

RiskMandate lays out three strengths, "honestly, in order": a behaviour policy ("not the strongest control, but a lot of value for the money, today"), hooks ("the rule checked on every call, outside the model's say-so"), and a boundary, SHarness ("the rule enforced by something the agent cannot reach").

Source: riskmandate.ai, read 24 Nov 2026.

The hook figure comes from a test on a chat-only agent whose rule was one line in the prompt. Told to ignore the rule, the line alone held 3 of 12 attacks; with a hook it held all 12, the hook blocking five. For SHarness RiskMandate says: "Six of six probes held on 25 October; nothing yet binds the agent itself."

SHarness turns an ABP into a policy "the Linux kernel holds on every process the agent starts". RiskMandate says it is "an early version" and marks parts as "design, not built". It also says: "We are still never in the request path."

Source: riskmandate.ai/secure-harness.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

The summit figures

What was not done

RiskMandate's own section, in its words: "Our summit ABPs were in observe mode by choice: gaps were logged and allowed, not blocked, apart from one call. Most were documented from each agent's brief and tools, not measured on it. Nobody outside the team has checked these counts. No customer is named, because none has agreed to be."

Source: riskmandate.ai, section 06, read 24 Nov 2026.

That one call: the newsroom's developer agent tried to write a hold file that only the security agent writes and only the lead lifts. It was the one call blocked in 2,262 runs.

Source: riskmandate.ai, situation of 6 Nov, read 24 Nov 2026.

The rule that failed

On 2 November a conductor agent pushed to main, though "never push to main" was written down as a sentence. On 6 November main was protected and the sentence became a barrier. This is the example RiskMandate itself uses of a rule becoming a technical barrier.

Source: web-summit.sgit.ai/abp/in-action/, read 24 Nov 2026.

Where they differ

  • Describe or decide. An ABP "describes and does not judge". TauGuard's core returns APPROVE, REVIEW or BLOCK.
  • In the path or beside it. RiskMandate: "We are still never in the request path." TauGuard: governance is "active at the execution boundary rather than reconstructed afterwards".
  • Observed or proven. RiskMandate's summit counts were observed in observe mode and "nobody outside the team has checked" them. TauGuard says its mechanisms can be examined "within their stated scope" and publishes a simulator for ARCHONT.

These differences are why the pairing could be useful. They are also the first things TauGuard may want to correct.

Four joint workflows

Each one is drawn, and each one is a proposal. None is agreed with TauGuard and none is built. Each says what it would take from TauGuard that we do not know it has.

W1. An ABP becomes predicates in TauGuard's core

Proposal RiskMandate writes an agent's ABP. TauGuard's core would evaluate each consequence-bearing call against predicates derived from it, and return APPROVE, REVIEW or BLOCK. Decisions go to the Chronicle and into the agent's run report.

What we know: TauGuard's governance page describes constitutional predicates, the three decisions and the Chronicle. RiskMandate's ABP lists the mandate row by row. What we do not know: whether a mandate row can be written as a predicate the core accepts, and how an outside agent's call reaches the core.

Source: tauguard.ai/governance.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

W2. The Sovereign Website change chain under ARCHONT

Proposal The Sovereign Website change chain (R4): brief, sites review, Factory request, security, decision, merge. Our public provenance is on the agents page. ARCHONT would hold each step as governed state. TauStamp would keep the lineage. Our provenance graph would be the evidence feed.

What we know: ARCHONT is described as maintaining governed state and TauStamp as preserving "governed lineage so that legitimacy can be reconstructed". What we do not know: how evidence from outside gets into ARCHONT, or whether the simulator TauGuard publishes can take it.

Source: tauguard.ai/archon.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

W3. PrompTugal News under TauDIL and SYGON

Proposal Before an article reaches the newsroom's own gate, TauDIL checks the semantic coherence of its claims, with SYGON. A refusal is terminal. Drift is reported. The newsroom's gate stays as it is.

What we know: SYGON's page says that where governance cannot establish an acceptable result, "the system can refuse rather than invent", and TauDIL's page offers an interactive demo. What we do not know: whether TauDIL can take news text, or what a coherence check needs as its "authorised knowledge".

Source: tauguard.ai/sygon.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

W4. TauGuard's own agents under RiskMandate ABPs

Proposal If TauGuard runs AI agents, RiskMandate would write an ABP for each, and TauGuard's core would be the boundary that stops what the ABP says should not happen. TauGuard's public pages do not say whether it runs agents; this workflow assumes it might.

This one is the plainest test of the whole idea: a governance company holding its own agents to its own boundary, with an outside party writing the policy. RiskMandate's caveat applies: its SHarness boundary has six of six probes held and nothing yet binds the agent itself.

Source: riskmandate.ai, read 24 Nov 2026.

A two-way MVP

One small thing that works in both directions, so each side learns what the other's design does under real use.

Proposal All of this is a proposal. Nothing here is agreed with TauGuard or built. The first thing we need from TauGuard is an endpoint or a demo we can call, and we do not assume it exists.

Ours: a keeper's push to dev

One of our agents, proposed as a keeper pushing to dev, acts only after a TauGuard APPROVE. REVIEW holds it for a person. BLOCK stops it, and the stop is final for that attempt. We chose this because the repository already has a rule that keepers push to dev only and a signing check, so we can compare TauGuard's decision with what the existing checks would have done.

Theirs: one article through TauDIL

One PrompTugal News article has its claims checked for semantic coherence by TauDIL, with SYGON, before the newsroom's own gate. We keep the gate. We see whether TauDIL's check agrees with it, and whether it reports drift.

What we would measure

MeasureMeaning
DecisionsHow many calls or claims were decided, and how each came out.
Correct and false blocksA block is correct if a person, looking later, agrees. A false block is one they overturn.
Added latencyThe time the check added to the push or the article.
PublishedWhat actually went out, and whether anything wrong did.

What we need from TauGuard

  • An endpoint or demo for an outside call. We do not assume one exists. TauGuard's pages offer a downloadable ARCHONT simulator and a TauDIL interactive demo; neither is known to take our input.
  • A statement of what TauGuard will agree to publish. We publish nothing about TauGuard's results without it.
  • Its own view of whether the two measurements are fair to its design.
Source: tauguard.ai/partners.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

Its partners page says pilots are "subject to technical qualification, scope, security requirements, commercial terms, data considerations and a mutually agreed written pilot agreement". This MVP is smaller than a pilot and would not replace one.

What next

TauGuard publishes its own path for working with others. We start from it, not from ours.

TauGuard's path

TauGuard's partners page sets out "From introduction to deployment" in five steps and says its objective is "to convert credible technical mechanisms into independently evaluated, commercially relevant deployments while keeping claims aligned with the evidence available."

Source: tauguard.ai/partners.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

The page names six kinds of contact, though it introduces them as "five ways": investors, sales channels, independent sales professionals, pilot deployment companies, strategic partners and enterprise evaluators.

Where we could fit

Proposal We are not investors or sellers. The nearest fits are the enterprise evaluator and the strategic partner, and then, if TauGuard wished, a small pilot.
