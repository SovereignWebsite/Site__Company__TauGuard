RiskMandate

RiskMandate's facts, as it states them, with its sources. We add nothing to the numbers and we repeat what it says was not done.

The ABP

An Agent Behaviour Policy "describes one AI agent in one deployment: everything it can do (the grant), what you actually authorised (the mandate), the gap between the two (the delta), and what really stands in the way of each thing (the barrier). It describes and it does not judge, so it carries no score."

Source: riskmandate.ai/agent-behaviour-policy.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

On its own team's pages the same four terms are Reach, Mandate, Gap and Barrier, and each barrier is labelled "a rule the agent keeps, a review by another agent or person, or a technical control the agent cannot change".

Source: web-summit.sgit.ai/abp/in-action/, read 24 Nov 2026.

Policy, hooks, boundary

RiskMandate lays out three strengths, "honestly, in order": a behaviour policy ("not the strongest control, but a lot of value for the money, today"), hooks ("the rule checked on every call, outside the model's say-so"), and a boundary, SHarness ("the rule enforced by something the agent cannot reach").

Source: riskmandate.ai, read 24 Nov 2026.

The hook figure comes from a test on a chat-only agent whose rule was one line in the prompt. Told to ignore the rule, the line alone held 3 of 12 attacks; with a hook it held all 12, the hook blocking five. For SHarness RiskMandate says: "Six of six probes held on 25 October; nothing yet binds the agent itself."

SHarness turns an ABP into a policy "the Linux kernel holds on every process the agent starts". RiskMandate says it is "an early version" and marks parts as "design, not built". It also says: "We are still never in the request path."

Source: riskmandate.ai/secure-harness.html, read 24 Nov 2026.

The summit figures

At Web Summit in November, RiskMandate counts "more than a hundred agents" on its own team, most with an ABP of their own.

191

behaviour policies written for RiskMandate's own agents and published on three sites (some agents appear on two). Evidence: 94 + 54 + 43, read 16 Nov.

2,262

agent runs, each with a report against its ABP. Evidence: the run reports, 6 to 16 Nov.

2,980

actions logged as outside an agent's grant: seen, recorded, allowed.

1

rule that failed and became a barrier the agent cannot remove. Evidence: the in-action record, 2 to 6 Nov.

94 of 109

agents on the team's circuit on 10 November had a written ABP (the stand deck).

Source: riskmandate.ai, read 24 Nov 2026.

What was not done

RiskMandate's own section, in its words: "Our summit ABPs were in observe mode by choice: gaps were logged and allowed, not blocked, apart from one call. Most were documented from each agent's brief and tools, not measured on it. Nobody outside the team has checked these counts. No customer is named, because none has agreed to be."

Source: riskmandate.ai, section 06, read 24 Nov 2026.

That one call: the newsroom's developer agent tried to write a hold file that only the security agent writes and only the lead lifts. It was the one call blocked in 2,262 runs.

Source: riskmandate.ai, situation of 6 Nov, read 24 Nov 2026.

The rule that failed

On 2 November a conductor agent pushed to main, though "never push to main" was written down as a sentence. On 6 November main was protected and the sentence became a barrier. This is the example RiskMandate itself uses of a rule becoming a technical barrier.

Source: web-summit.sgit.ai/abp/in-action/, read 24 Nov 2026.
